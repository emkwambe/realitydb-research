# Included Document: RESEARCH-CHARTER.md

# RealityDB Research Charter
Version: 1.0
Status: Active

---

# Purpose

This repository exists to discover products through disciplined research rather than invention.

We do not begin with products.

We begin with evidence.

Products emerge only after recurring problems, unmet needs, and commercially viable capabilities have been demonstrated through independent investigation.

---

# Mission

Conduct rigorous, evidence-based product discovery that identifies opportunities worth building.

The objective is not to prove ideas correct.

The objective is to discover whether they deserve to exist.

---

# Research Philosophy

We believe that:

Evidence is superior to opinion.

Repeated observations are stronger than isolated examples.

Contradictions are more valuable than agreement.

Blind spots are opportunities.

A rejected hypothesis is a successful research outcome.

The cost of killing a weak idea early is significantly lower than building the wrong product.

---

# Guiding Principles

## 1. Evidence Before Conclusions

Every significant conclusion must be supported by evidence.

Conclusions without supporting evidence are hypotheses.

---

## 2. Observation Before Interpretation

Separate:

What was observed

from

What it may mean.

Never confuse facts with interpretations.

---

## 3. Preserve Contradictions

Contradictory findings must never be discarded.

Contradictions often reveal:

- hidden customer segments
- market fragmentation
- different buying behaviors
- incorrect assumptions
- emerging opportunities

---

## 4. Preserve Uncertainty

If evidence is insufficient, explicitly state:

"We do not know."

Research exists to reduce uncertainty—not eliminate it.

---

## 5. Product Neutrality

Researchers must never become emotionally attached to a product direction.

The goal is discovery.

Not validation.

---

## 6. Capability Before Product

Research first identifies:

problems

↓

recurring capabilities

↓

commercial capabilities

↓

products

A capability does not automatically justify a product.

---

## 7. Commercial Reality

Technical elegance alone is insufficient.

Every proposed capability must eventually answer:

Who buys this?

Why now?

Why us?

Why not an existing solution?

---

Research is not successful because it produces a product.

# Investigator Independence

Each investigator performs research independently.

Investigators must not:

Coordinate findings

Copy conclusions

Seek consensus

Optimize for agreement

Independent thinking produces stronger evidence.

# Research Director

The Research Director does not perform primary research.

The Research Director:

The Research Director is responsible for protecting the organization from weak conclusions.

---

# Decision Rule

Agreement does not determine truth.

Popularity does not determine truth.

Confidence does not determine truth.

Evidence determines truth.

---

# Default Assumption

Assume every hypothesis is incomplete until sufficient evidence proves otherwise.

The burden of proof always belongs to the hypothesis.

---

# Standard of Proof

A recommendation should not advance toward product definition unless:

the problem is recurring

the customer is identifiable

the economic value is meaningful

existing alternatives are understood

adoption barriers are known

commercial viability is plausible

critical assumptions have been investigated

If these conditions are not met:

Research continues.

\# Research Standards

Version: 1.0

Status: Active



\---



\# Purpose



These standards define the minimum quality required for all research conducted within this repository.



Every investigator must follow these standards regardless of research topic, methodology, or personal conclusions.



\---



\# Research Objectives



Every investigation should reduce uncertainty.



Research should answer questions, expose assumptions, discover patterns, and identify commercially meaningful opportunities.



Research should never be performed simply to confirm an existing belief.



\---



\# Research Quality Standards



Every report must be:



Accurate



Traceable



Evidence-based



Reproducible



Commercially relevant



Transparent about uncertainty



\---



\# Every Claim Requires Evidence



Claims must be supported by one or more identifiable sources.



Avoid statements such as:



"Most companies..."



"Everyone is moving toward..."



"The market wants..."



Unless those statements are supported by evidence.



\---



\# Separate Four Things



Never mix these categories.



\## Observation



A directly supported fact.



Example:



"Seven of ten AI governance platforms provide model evaluation."



\---



\## Interpretation



An explanation of what an observation may imply.



Example:



"Evaluation appears to be becoming a standard capability."



\---



\## Assumption



Something believed but not yet demonstrated.



Example:



"Buyers may prefer integrated platforms."



\---



\## Recommendation



A proposed action based on available evidence.



Example:



"Investigate integrated evaluation platforms before defining product scope."



\---



\# Preserve Original Evidence



Never rewrite quotations.



Never modify source statistics.



Never remove contradictory findings.



Evidence should remain available for later verification.



\---



\# Research Transparency



Every report should clearly distinguish:



Known facts



Strong evidence



Weak evidence



Assumptions



Unknowns



Questions requiring further investigation



\---



\# Handle Contradictions Explicitly



If sources disagree:



Do not average them.



Do not ignore either side.



Document:



What conflicts



Possible reasons



Evidence supporting each position



Remaining uncertainty



\---



\# Avoid Confirmation Bias



Investigators must actively search for evidence that disproves their own emerging conclusions.



The strongest report is not the one with the clearest story.



The strongest report is the one that survives attempts to disprove it.



\---



\# Research Scope



Investigators should explore adjacent topics when necessary.



Unexpected findings are often more valuable than expected ones.



However, unrelated exploration should be documented separately rather than mixed into the primary investigation.



\---



\# Commercial Perspective



Research should continually ask:



Who experiences this problem?



How often?



How expensive is it?



Who pays for solving it?



What alternatives exist?



Why are existing solutions insufficient?



\---



\# Technical Perspective



Investigators should identify:



Current practices



Technical constraints



Integration requirements



Operational complexity



Security considerations



Scalability implications



Maintenance burden



\---



\# Confidence Levels



Every major conclusion should include one of four confidence levels.



High



Supported by multiple independent sources with little contradiction.



Moderate



Supported by several sources but with some uncertainty.



Low



Limited evidence or significant disagreement.



Unknown



Insufficient evidence to draw conclusions.



\---



\# Required Questions



Every investigation should attempt to answer:



What problem exists?



Who experiences it?



Why does it exist?



Why hasn't it been solved?



How is it solved today?



Why are current solutions insufficient?



What capability repeatedly appears?



Does that capability justify a product?



What evidence contradicts this conclusion?



What remains unknown?



\---



\# Documentation Standard



Research should enable another investigator to independently verify every major conclusion.



A report that cannot be independently verified is incomplete.



\---



\# Research Ethics



Do not fabricate evidence.



Do not omit contradictory findings.



Do not exaggerate certainty.



Do not selectively cite sources.



Represent uncertainty honestly.



\---



\# Completion Standard



Research is complete only when:



Evidence has been documented.



Contradictions have been preserved.



Unknowns have been identified.



Recommendations are supported by evidence.



The report can be independently reviewed.



Otherwise:



Research remains in progress.

\# Source Hierarchy

Version: 1.0

Status: Active



\---



\# Purpose



Not all sources carry equal evidentiary weight.



This document establishes the order of trust assigned to different types of evidence used throughout this repository.



Higher-ranked sources should always be preferred when available.



Lower-ranked sources may still provide valuable insight but require stronger corroboration.



\---



\# Principle



Research quality depends more on evidence quality than evidence quantity.



Ten weak sources do not outweigh one authoritative source.



\---



\# Tier S — Primary Evidence



Highest confidence.



Generated directly from the organization, technology, or market being investigated.



Examples:



Official product documentation



Technical architecture documentation



Engineering blogs from product teams



Source code repositories



API documentation



Standards specifications



Government regulations



Regulatory filings



Patent filings



Official pricing



Official product demonstrations



Official release notes



Public technical presentations by engineering teams



Preferred use:



Support factual claims.



\---



\# Tier A — Direct Market Evidence



Very high confidence.



Evidence generated through actual commercial activity.



Examples:



Customer case studies



Enterprise implementation reports



Procurement documentation



RFP responses



Migration guides



Customer engineering documentation



Architecture diagrams



Public incident reports



Conference presentations



Recorded technical talks



Industry benchmark reports



Preferred use:



Understanding real-world usage.



\---



\# Tier B — Independent Analysis



High confidence when methodology is transparent.



Examples:



Academic papers



Peer-reviewed publications



Independent research organizations



Technical analysts



Industry whitepapers



Open benchmark studies



University research



Preferred use:



Understanding broader industry patterns.



\---



\# Tier C — Market Signals



Moderate confidence.



Useful for identifying recurring themes but not sufficient alone.



Examples:



Job postings



Hiring trends



Conference agendas



Product roadmaps



Feature announcements



Partner announcements



Community discussions



Developer surveys



Preferred use:



Detect emerging capabilities and demand.



\---



\# Tier D — Community Knowledge



Moderate to low confidence.



Useful for discovering operational reality and implementation challenges.



Examples:



GitHub Issues



Stack Overflow



Reddit



Hacker News



Discord



Community forums



Developer blogs



Independent consultants



Preferred use:



Operational insight.



Never use as sole evidence for strategic conclusions.



\---



\# Tier E — Opinion



Lowest confidence.



Examples:



Personal blogs



Social media



Podcasts



Interviews



News commentary



Editorials



Marketing material



Vendor claims without evidence



Preferred use:



Generate hypotheses.



Never use as supporting evidence for major conclusions.



\---



\# Evidence Ranking Rules



Whenever multiple sources exist:



Prefer:



Primary evidence



↓



Independent evidence



↓



Community evidence



↓



Opinion



Never reverse this order without justification.



\---



\# Independent Confirmation



Strong conclusions should ideally be supported by:



Multiple independent sources



Different source types



Different organizations



Independent confirmation is stronger than repeated reporting of the same original source.



\---



\# Source Diversity



Avoid relying on one organization.



A high-quality report should include evidence from multiple companies, industries, and perspectives whenever practical.



\---



\# Commercial Balance



Research should balance:



Technical sources



Business sources



Customer sources



Regulatory sources



Operational sources



A technically correct conclusion may still fail commercially.



\---



\# Citation Requirements



Every important claim should be traceable to its original source.



Whenever possible record:



Title



Organization



Author



Publication date



URL



Date accessed



Supporting quotation or relevant excerpt



\---



\# Evidence Conflicts



When higher-ranked evidence conflicts with lower-ranked evidence:



Prefer the higher-ranked source.



However, preserve the contradiction for future investigation.



Contradictions often reveal market transitions or differences between theory and practice.



\---



\# Source Quality Checklist



Before citing a source ask:



Who created it?



Why was it created?



Is there commercial bias?



Can the claim be independently verified?



Is it current?



Is it technically accurate?



Does it directly support the claim?



\---



\# Repository Rule



Every significant recommendation should ultimately trace back to at least one Tier S, Tier A, or Tier B source whenever possible.



If only Tier C, D, or E sources exist, explicitly state that stronger evidence is unavailable.



\---



\# Final Principle



The credibility of this repository is determined not by how much evidence it contains, but by the quality, independence, and traceability of that evidence.

"Correlation of sources is not independence of sources."



For example, if ten articles all quote the same OpenAI blog post, you still have one primary source—not ten independent confirmations.

# Included Document: EVIDENCE-SCORING.md

\# Evidence Scoring

Version: 1.0

Status: Active



\---



\# Purpose



Evidence is not binary.



Evidence exists on a spectrum of strength.



This document defines how investigators evaluate the quality, reliability, and commercial significance of evidence.



Scores are intended to improve decision-making, not replace judgment.



\---



\# Principle



Research should be driven by the strongest evidence available.



Evidence scores communicate confidence—not certainty.



\---



\# Scoring Dimensions



Each significant piece of evidence should be evaluated across six dimensions.



Each dimension is scored from 1 to 5.



\---



\## 1. Source Authority



How authoritative is the source?



1 — Anonymous or unverifiable



2 — Personal opinion



3 — Independent publication



4 — Industry or academic authority



5 — Official primary source



\---



\## 2. Evidence Quality



How directly does the evidence support the claim?



1 — Speculation



2 — Anecdotal observation



3 — Indirect support



4 — Direct supporting evidence



5 — Direct measurement or documented implementation



\---



\## 3. Independence



How independent is the evidence?



1 — Repeated reporting of the same source



2 — Similar derivative sources



3 — Partially independent



4 — Multiple independent organizations



5 — Independent confirmation from unrelated sources



\---



\## 4. Commercial Relevance



How meaningful is this evidence for product decisions?



1 — Interesting



2 — Potentially relevant



3 — Useful



4 — Important



5 — Critical commercial evidence



\---



\## 5. Recurrence



How often does the same observation appear?



1 — Single occurrence



2 — Rare



3 — Occasional



4 — Common



5 — Consistently recurring across industries



\---



\## 6. Timeliness



How current is the evidence?



1 — Obsolete



2 — Older but still referenced



3 — Moderately current



4 — Current



5 — Represents the latest known state



\---



\# Overall Evidence Score



Maximum score:



30



Suggested interpretation:



26–30



Very Strong Evidence



Supports major decisions.



\---



21–25



Strong Evidence



Reliable with limited uncertainty.



\---



16–20



Moderate Evidence



Useful but requires corroboration.



\---



11–15



Weak Evidence



Generate hypotheses only.



\---



6–10



Very Weak Evidence



Do not use for strategic conclusions.



\---



\# Commercial Weight



Some evidence changes product direction more than others.



Increase attention when evidence directly affects:



Customer willingness to pay



Market demand



Regulatory requirements



Integration complexity



Competitive differentiation



Operational cost



Adoption barriers



Switching costs



Buyer identity



These factors deserve additional discussion regardless of numerical score.



\---



\# Contradictory Evidence



High-scoring contradictory evidence should never be ignored.



Instead:



Document both findings.



Explain the disagreement.



Recommend additional investigation.



\---



\# Missing Evidence



Absence of evidence is not evidence of absence.



If important evidence cannot be located:



Record it.



Treat it as research debt.



Generate a follow-up mission if necessary.



\---



\# Research Debt



Examples:



Unknown buyer



Unknown pricing



Unknown procurement process



Unknown implementation effort



Unknown regulatory impact



Unknown competitors



Unknown adoption barriers



Research debt should be tracked until resolved.



\---



\# Evidence Aging



Evidence loses value over time.



Investigators should verify that critical evidence remains current before relying upon it.



Older evidence may still provide historical context but should not drive strategic recommendations without validation.



\---



\# Decision Thresholds



A product hypothesis should not advance solely because it has many sources.



Instead ask:



Is the evidence independent?



Is the evidence recent?



Is the evidence commercially meaningful?



Is the evidence repeatable?



Does contradictory evidence exist?



\---



\# Repository Rule



Every major recommendation should include:



Evidence score



Confidence level



Remaining uncertainty



Known assumptions



Outstanding research debt



\---



\# Final Principle



Strong evidence reduces uncertainty.



It does not eliminate it.



Research ends not when every question has an answer, but when the remaining uncertainty is understood well enough to make an informed decision.

\# Source Quality



Apply the source hierarchy defined by the RealityDB Research System.



Prefer:



Tier S sources for binding requirements, official records, and verified primary evidence.



Tier A sources for authoritative technical, commercial, and operational evidence.



Tier B sources for credible supporting evidence.



Use Tier C sources cautiously.



Tier D and Tier E sources may identify leads but cannot independently justify major strategic conclusions.



\---



\# Source Independence



Multiple sources do not automatically represent multiple independent evidence chains.



Treat the following as a single evidence chain when they repeat the same originating claim:



\- syndicated articles

\- vendor press releases republished by media outlets

\- analyst reports derived from the same survey

\- blogs quoting the same customer announcement

\- job-posting aggregators reproducing one original posting

\- multiple summaries of one regulatory document

\- social posts linking to one original source



Investigators must identify the likely original source whenever possible.
